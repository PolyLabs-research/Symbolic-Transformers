# Magnitude comparison, held-out band off-centre (350-449)

Generated by `python scripts/magnitude_experiment.py --out-dir runs/magnitude_offcenter --holdout 350-449 --seeds 1 --epochs 30`
plus `--arms sinusoid`, `--arms sinusoid_smooth`, and `--arms grid_linear sinusoid sinusoid_smooth --seeds 2 3`.
Raw numbers: `magnitude_offcenter.json`.

Same task and budget as `magnitude_3seeds.md` (values 0-499, 20k training pairs, tiny model, 30 epochs) with
one change: the held-out band is 350-449 instead of 200-299, so an unknown token can no longer be answered
by treating it as "roughly average". Five arms; three seeds for `grid_linear` and both sinusoids, one seed
for `grid_conv` and `learned_untied`:

| arm | numeral embedding | what it tests |
|---|---|---|
| `learned_untied` | learned row per token | the original model |
| `grid_linear` | `Linear(625)` over the thermometer panel | nested code |
| `grid_conv` | conv over the 25×25 panel | nested code, read as a shape |
| `sinusoid` | `Linear(64)` over sin/cos of *k*, periods 2-1250 | ordinal code, no nesting |
| `sinusoid_smooth` | same, periods 32-1250 | smooth ordinal code, no nesting |

## Reading

1. **The centring artifact is gone and the grid result stands.** `learned_untied` drops from 86.7% (centred
   band) to 61.7% on held-out pairs, and from 95.5% to 63.2% when one operand is unseen. `grid_linear` is
   93.8 ± 0.7% overall and 99.3% with one unseen operand; `grid_conv` 91.8% / 97.9%.

2. **Both operands unseen, gap over 20 (three seeds):** grid_linear 81.7 ± 5.3%, sinusoid 57.7 ± 9.5%
   (seeds: 52, 71, 50), sinusoid_smooth 58.9 ± 1.3%, learned 54.2%. Only the panel arm is reliably above
   chance. Equal unseen pairs are ~100% for every fixed-feature arm (identical inputs) and 89% for the
   learned table.

3. **Magnitude readout on unseen values (Spearman, three seeds):** grid_linear 1.000 ± 0.000 with a mean
   error of 5.5 units in every seed; sinusoid 0.86 ± 0.06 (errors 10-27); sinusoid_smooth 0.56 ± 0.19
   (errors 17-29); learned 0.18. The thermometer places an unseen value correctly whatever the seed; the
   Fourier codes sometimes do and sometimes do not.

4. **Combined with the centred run, the claim is specific.** On a gap bracketed by trained values on both
   sides (`magnitude_3seeds.md`) the sinusoid matched the grid. On a gap at the edge of the trained range it
   does not. So the thing the panel adds is not smoothness in *k* (the smooth sinusoid is worse, not better)
   but nesting: an unseen panel is a subset of trained panels, its cells only ever receive gradient as a block
   from panels above the gap, and the embedding walks monotonically through the gap regardless of where the
   gap sits. A Fourier basis fitted on one side of a gap is free to wander inside it.

5. **Beyond the trained range nothing works**: panel cells above 499 never received gradient, and the
   sinusoid readouts invert (far Spearman about −0.9 for every fixed-feature arm). Extrapolation needs an
   encoder with shared structure across cell positions, not 625 independent columns.

6. **Precision inside the gap is still coarse** for the panel arms: gaps under about 20 are at chance.

7. **`grid_shared` extrapolates order but inverts inside the gap.** Far band: 88.0 ± 0.4% overall, 100% when
   one operand is far (judged larger than every trained value), chance between two far values. Held-out
   gap: 44.8 ± 4.4% on two-unseen pairs and 33.7 ± 6.1% at gaps over 20, below chance, against 72.6% and
   81.7% for grid_linear. Same cause as in the centred run: row/column features and index periods down to
   32 let the generated increments oscillate inside the gap. A smoother basis is the follow-up.
