# Magnitude comparison, three seeds

Generated by `python scripts/magnitude_experiment.py --out-dir runs/magnitude --seeds 1 2 3 --epochs 30`.
Raw numbers: `magnitude_3seeds.json` (one `evaluate_magnitude.py` report per arm and seed).

**Task.** `MESSAGE_START NUM_a <op> NUM_b ANSWER -> TRUE | FALSE`, ops LESS_THAN / GREATER_THAN, 2% of pairs
with a = b. Operands are drawn from 0-499 with
200-299 held out of training; val/test pairs are disjoint from
training pairs; `test_holdout` pairs have at least one operand in the held-out band, `test_far` at least one in
500-624 (beyond the trained range). 20000 training pairs,
tiny model, batch 64, lr 0.0003, 40 epochs, seeds 1-3. Arms: `learned_untied` (the
original model), `learned` (tied output), `grid_linear` and `grid_conv` (tied output), and the `sinusoid`
control (fixed sin/cos features of k, periods 2-1250, through the same linear layer and tied output; added
after the first four arms ran).

## Reading

1. **Everyone solves the task on values it trained on** (97-99% on disjoint test pairs). The grid arms are
   sharper on close pairs: 86% at |a-b| = 1 against 61-67% for learned tables, and 76% vs 55% at gaps 2-5.
   The thermometer makes a one-cell difference explicit; a learned table has to infer it from co-occurrence.

2. **Grid embeddings carry magnitude for values that were never trained; learned tables carry none.**
   A linear readout of k fitted on training-pool numerals and applied to the held-out band gives Spearman
   1.00 and a mean error of about 9 units for `grid_linear` (0.94 and 10 for `grid_conv`); for both learned
   tables it is about 0 (R² far below zero). That is the thermometer code doing exactly what it should: the
   panel for an unseen k is a subset of panels the model did train on.

3. **In the task, with both operands unseen, only the grid arms are above chance.** `grid_linear` answers
   78 ± 10% of such comparisons with a gap over 20 and 100% of equal pairs; `grid_conv` 58%; both learned
   tables sit at 50% for every gap. The precision is coarse, though: gaps below about 20 are at chance for
   the grid arms too, consistent with the ~10-unit readout error above.

4. **One design artifact to discount.** The held-out band sits in the middle of the range, so any model that
   treats an unknown token as "roughly average" answers most *one-unseen* pairs correctly without knowing
   anything. `learned_untied` does exactly that: 95.5% on one-unseen pairs, chance on two-unseen pairs, no
   magnitude in its table. The one-unseen columns are therefore not evidence about magnitude; the
   two-unseen rows and the readout probe are. A follow-up run moves the band to 350-449 to remove the
   artifact (`experiments/magnitude_offcenter_seed1.md` when present).

5. **Nothing extrapolates beyond the trained range, and the grid arms fail there systematically**, scoring
   below chance on far pairs with large gaps. The reason is specific: cell i of a panel only receives
   gradient from panels with k > i, so the projection columns for cells 500-624 were never trained and an
   unseen panel that reaches into them points in a random direction. The learned projection of the
   thermometer interpolates; it does not extrapolate. An encoder whose per-cell weights are a smooth
   function of cell position (or a fixed structured projection) is the lever for that.

6. **Tying the output to a learned table hurts again**: `learned` (tied) is at chance on every held-out
   breakdown including gap 0, where `learned_untied` gets 86%.

7. **The sinusoid control matches the grid on this centred gap.** `sinusoid` reaches 93.7 ± 0.1% on held-out
   pairs (grid_linear 93.9 ± 1.6), 73.5 ± 0.9% with both operands unseen (72.4 ± 7.3), 81 ± 1% at gaps over 20
   (78 ± 10), and a magnitude readout of 0.955 ± 0.017 on unseen values (1.000). So for a gap bracketed by
   trained values on both sides, any ordinal code the linear layer can interpolate does as well as the
   thermometer; the panel is not uniquely responsible. With the band moved to the edge of the trained
   range (350-449, `magnitude_offcenter.md`, three seeds) the sinusoid drops to 57.7 ± 9.5% on both-unseen
   comparisons over gap 20 and the smooth sinusoid to 58.9 ± 1.3%, while grid_linear holds at 81.7 ± 5.3%
   with a readout of 1.000 in every seed. Nesting, not smoothness, is what the grid adds: it interpolates a
   gap wherever the gap sits, a Fourier code only where the gap is bracketed on both sides.

8. **`grid_shared` (cell weights generated from smooth position features) extrapolates order but inverts
   inside the gap.** On the far band it scores 86.5 ± 3.5% overall and 99.7% when one operand is far: a value
   beyond the trained range is judged larger than any trained value, which no other arm manages. Two far
   values against each other are at chance (47 ± 13%): the continuation is monotone but saturated. Inside the
   held-out gap, however, two-unseen comparisons are *below* chance (35.0 ± 1.0%, 19.9% at gaps over 20),
   while one-unseen pairs stay at 98.6%. The basis is the culprit: row/column features oscillate every 25
   cells along the fill order and index periods go down to 32, so increments inside a 100-wide gap can
   change sign while only their sum is constrained. A smoother basis (and a per-cell residual for precision
   on trained cells) is the direct follow-up.

9. **A smoother basis does not fix the inversion, and a per-cell residual does not help either.**
   `grid_smooth` (index features only, periods ≥ 128) keeps the extrapolation (87.5 ± 6.1% on the far band,
   99.0% with one far operand) but is just as inverted inside the gap: 35.8 ± 2.3% on two-unseen pairs,
   20.6% at gaps over 20. `grid_hybrid` (same plus a zero-initialised per-cell residual) is inverted too
   (30.8 ± 4.3%) and gives back some extrapolation (73.4 ± 1.4%), because the residuals on trained cells
   carry magnitude that the smooth part then lacks. Neither improves close-pair precision on seen values
   (gap 1: 80-83% vs 87% for grid_linear). The linear readout of *k* on the held-out band is 1.000 for all
   three shared variants, so the gap *is* linearly ordered in the table; the model's comparator simply does
   not read it that way for values it never trained on. The practical summary: per-cell weights trained as
   a block (grid_linear) interpolate but cannot extrapolate; weights generated from a shared function of
   position extrapolate order but invert inside a gap; nothing tried does both.

10. **An encoder that does both: `grid_ramp_res`.** Two fixed directions per cell, [1, i/625], generated
    through one shared matrix, plus the zero-initialised per-cell residual. The panel embedding is
    k·a + (k²/1250)·c + b + (residuals of filled cells): a curve whose direction rotates uniformly with k, so
    the walk through a gap is a straight segment of the same curve by construction and the continuation past
    the trained range is too. Held-out gap: 94.3 ± 1.0% overall, 75.5 ± 4.6% on two-unseen pairs, 82.8 ± 6.0%
    at gaps over 20 (grid_linear: 93.9 / 72.4 / 78.1). Far band: 75.9 ± 3.6% overall, 93.3 ± 3.1% with one far
    operand (grid_linear 46.8 / 49.7; grid_shared 86.5 / 99.7). Readout of k: 1.00 on the gap, 0.14 ± 0.84 on
    the far band (seed-dependent), but the task accuracy says the order is carried. The cost is close-pair
    precision on seen values: 83% at gap 1 and 77% at gaps 2-5 against 87% / 76% for grid_linear, and a few
    equal-pair errors.

11. **Two negative results on the way there, both informative.** `grid_tied` (basis [1] only: every cell the
    same weight, embedding = k × one direction) sat at 51% on every split for every seed, with or without the
    residual: LayerNorm is scale-invariant, so k·u normalises to the same vector for every k > 0 (cosine
    1.0000 at init), and identical embeddings give identical gradients to every cell, so the residual cannot
    break the symmetry. `grid_ramp` without the residual also stayed at chance (50.8%): the rotating path is
    there (readout 1.000 everywhere) but adjacent values are too close for the comparator to learn from in
    30 epochs. The residual is what makes the smooth path learnable; the ramp is what makes it continue.
