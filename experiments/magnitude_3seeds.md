# Magnitude comparison, three seeds

Generated by `python scripts/magnitude_experiment.py --out-dir runs/magnitude --seeds 1 2 3 --epochs 30`.
Raw numbers: `magnitude_3seeds.json` (one `evaluate_magnitude.py` report per arm and seed).

**Task.** `MESSAGE_START NUM_a <op> NUM_b ANSWER -> TRUE | FALSE`, ops LESS_THAN / GREATER_THAN, 2% of pairs
with a = b. Operands are drawn from 0-499 with
200-299 held out of training; val/test pairs are disjoint from
training pairs; `test_holdout` pairs have at least one operand in the held-out band, `test_far` at least one in
500-624 (beyond the trained range). 20000 training pairs,
tiny model, batch 64, lr 0.0003, 40 epochs, seeds 1-3. Arms: `learned_untied` (the
original model), `learned` (tied output), `grid_linear` and `grid_conv` (tied output).

## Reading

1. **Everyone solves the task on values it trained on** (97-99% on disjoint test pairs). The grid arms are
   sharper on close pairs: 86% at |a-b| = 1 against 61-67% for learned tables, and 76% vs 55% at gaps 2-5.
   The thermometer makes a one-cell difference explicit; a learned table has to infer it from co-occurrence.

2. **Grid embeddings carry magnitude for values that were never trained; learned tables carry none.**
   A linear readout of k fitted on training-pool numerals and applied to the held-out band gives Spearman
   1.00 and a mean error of about 9 units for `grid_linear` (0.94 and 10 for `grid_conv`); for both learned
   tables it is about 0 (R² far below zero). That is the thermometer code doing exactly what it should: the
   panel for an unseen k is a subset of panels the model did train on.

3. **In the task, with both operands unseen, only the grid arms are above chance.** `grid_linear` answers
   78 ± 10% of such comparisons with a gap over 20 and 100% of equal pairs; `grid_conv` 58%; both learned
   tables sit at 50% for every gap. The precision is coarse, though: gaps below about 20 are at chance for
   the grid arms too, consistent with the ~10-unit readout error above.

4. **One design artifact to discount.** The held-out band sits in the middle of the range, so any model that
   treats an unknown token as "roughly average" answers most *one-unseen* pairs correctly without knowing
   anything. `learned_untied` does exactly that: 95.5% on one-unseen pairs, chance on two-unseen pairs, no
   magnitude in its table. The one-unseen columns are therefore not evidence about magnitude; the
   two-unseen rows and the readout probe are. A follow-up run moves the band to 350-449 to remove the
   artifact (`experiments/magnitude_offcenter_seed1.md` when present).

5. **Nothing extrapolates beyond the trained range, and the grid arms fail there systematically**, scoring
   below chance on far pairs with large gaps. The reason is specific: cell i of a panel only receives
   gradient from panels with k > i, so the projection columns for cells 500-624 were never trained and an
   unseen panel that reaches into them points in a random direction. The learned projection of the
   thermometer interpolates; it does not extrapolate. An encoder whose per-cell weights are a smooth
   function of cell position (or a fixed structured projection) is the lever for that.

6. **Tying the output to a learned table hurts again**: `learned` (tied) is at chance on every held-out
   breakdown including gap 0, where `learned_untied` gets 86%.
