# Magnitude comparison, precision: close-pair-enriched data

Data generated by `python scripts/magnitude_experiment.py --out-dir runs/magnitude_precision --holdout 350-449
--small-gap-frac 0.4 --small-gap-max 10 --epochs 40 --seeds 1 2 3`; the later batches reuse the same data
directory. Raw numbers for every arm: `magnitude_precision.json`.

Same layout as `magnitude_offcenter.md` (values 0-499, 350-449 held out, 500-624 never seen) but 40% of
pairs are drawn with |a − b| ≤ 10, so gaps of 1-10 make up 35% of training instead of about 2%, and the
both-unseen held-out bins at small gaps hold ~1,400 pairs instead of a few dozen. 40 epochs unless stated.

Arms, all `grid_shared` with the ramp basis and a per-cell residual unless named otherwise:

| arm | residual | extra |
|---|---|---|
| `learned_untied`, `grid_linear` | – | controls from the earlier studies |
| `grid_ramp_res` | lr ×1, weight decay 0.01 | |
| `grid_ramp_res_mid` / `_fast` | lr ×3, wd 0.03 / lr ×10, wd 0.1 | stronger residual |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth`, `_mid_orth`, `_fast_orth` | as above | residual projected off the ramp plane (`--residual-orthogonal`) |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_80ep` | lr ×1, orthogonal | 80 epochs |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_drop` | lr ×1, orthogonal | half the training batches embed with the ramp alone (`--residual-dropout 0.5`) |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_cdrop` | lr ×1, orthogonal | each cell's residual row dropped independently (`--residual-dropout-cell`) |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_drop_80ep` | lr ×1, orthogonal, dropout 0.5 | 80 epochs |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal` | lr ×1, orthogonal | residual scaled 1 → 0 linearly between 30% and 70% of training (`--residual-anneal 0.3 0.7`), 80 epochs; best epoch from the residual-free phase |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_geo` | lr ×1, orthogonal | as above but geometric, 1 → 0.001 then 0 (`--residual-anneal-floor 0.001`) |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_geo_long` | lr ×1, orthogonal | the same anneal at epochs 24-56 of a 120-epoch run: 64 residual-free epochs |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_hold` | lr ×1, orthogonal | geometric 1 → 0.01 over the window, then held at 0.01 (`--residual-anneal-hold`) |

## Reading

1. **Close-pair training buys seen precision, as intended.** grid_linear goes from 83% to 98.8% at gaps 2-5
   and holds 85.6% at gap 1; the ramp with the fast residual reaches 90.2% at gap 1 and 99.7% at gaps 2-5.
   The learned table reaches 58% / 77%.

2. **And it destroys interpolation for every fine-structure encoder.** On both-unseen held-out pairs over
   gap 20, grid_linear falls from 81.7% (uniform data) to 1.4 ± 1.6%, and the fast residual to 2.1 ± 1.4%:
   not chance, systematically inverted. Their comparators learned to read per-cell texture, and cells that
   were never trained have none (grid_linear) or only the block pattern, which reads backwards.

3. **Residual strength is a dial between the two.** With the residual kept off the magnitude plane, going
   from lr ×1 to ×3 to ×10 moves held-out accuracy 88.1 → 70.6 → 61.8% and both-unseen gap 6-20 accuracy
   81.7 → 44.6 → 22.4%, while seen gap-1 accuracy goes 80.7 → 85.9 → 86.2%. The orthogonal projection helps
   at the slow and middle settings (held-out 88.1 vs 83.4%, 70.6 vs 65.4%; far band 78.9 vs 72.1%, 73.3 vs
   65.1%); at the fast setting the residual dominates whichever way it points.

4. **Longer training is the same trade, not a way out.** 80 epochs of the slow orthogonal residual give the
   best seen precision of the study (92.0 ± 6.1% at gap 1, 99.5% at gaps 2-5, validation loss 0.037) and
   give back the unseen band: both-unseen held-out pairs at gaps 6-20 fall from 81.7% to 36.3%, and over gap
   20 from 100% to 11.9%, inverted again. The embedding has not lost magnitude (linear readout of k still
   transfers to the held-out band at Spearman 1.000 and to the far band at 0.903): the comparator stopped
   reading it. The residual simply keeps growing (per-cell norm 3.4 → 3.9 for seed 1, against 5.5 for the
   generated part).

5. **No model so far has a magnitude route through the ramp alone.** Zeroing the residual of a trained
   checkpoint (slow orthogonal, 40 or 80 epochs) drops it to chance on every pair, seen ones included
   (test 41-53%). The residual is not a refinement on top of a working shared code; it is a large part of
   every embedding and the comparator is tuned to the sum, so the "unseen" failure is the comparator
   meeting a sum whose residual half it was never trained on.

6. **Residual dropout is the best configuration so far, on every unseen measure, at no cost on seen
   pairs.** Embedding half of the training batches with the ramp alone (`grid_ramp_res_orth_drop`) gives
   test 98.6 ± 0.3% (gap 1 81.0 ± 3.8%, gaps 2-5 95.3 ± 1.7%, the same as the slow orthogonal residual),
   held-out 91.9 ± 2.2% with both-unseen pairs at 83.8 ± 4.3% (gaps 6-20 94.0 ± 2.9%, over gap 20 100%),
   and far band 88.5 ± 3.6% with both-unseen pairs at 77.6 ± 7.0% (gaps 6-20 82.4 ± 9.1%, over gap 20
   100%). The linear readout of k transfers to both bands at Spearman 1.000 and PC1 tracks k on all three.
   It does not work the way it was designed to: training loss stays near 0.46 while validation loss (residual
   intact) reaches 0.08, so the ramp-only batches sit at chance and the model learned to be indifferent to
   them rather than to read them, and zeroing the residual still gives chance. What changed is the residual
   itself: per-cell norm 1.0-1.2 instead of 3.4 (generated part 3.2 instead of 5.6), a third of the shared
   part instead of 60%, and a comparator trained on a small residual reads untrained cells' generated
   weights correctly. The per-cell variant (`_cdrop`) never trained: validation loss 0.67 after 40 epochs
   in every seed.

7. **Training the dropout arm longer trades the held-out band for the far band.** At 80 epochs
   (`grid_ramp_res_orth_drop_80ep`, validation loss 0.035 ± 0.003, best epochs 75-79) seen precision reaches
   the level of the 80-epoch run without dropout (91.7 ± 2.0% at gap 1, 99.4 ± 0.4% at gaps 2-5), and the
   far band becomes the best unseen result of the study: 96.3 ± 1.9% overall, both-unseen pairs 92.8 ± 3.8%,
   with 78.1 ± 15.5% at gap 1, 86.0 ± 10.5% at gaps 2-5 and 99.5% at gaps 6-20 (seed 1 is at 100% from gap 1
   up). The held-out band goes the other way: 75.9 ± 8.0% overall, both-unseen pairs 52.3 ± 15.6%, and two
   of three seeds invert over gap 20 (32% and 9%; the third keeps 100%). The mechanism is the one from
   point 4 in a milder form. The ramp-only batches are still not learned (training loss 0.40 against
   validation 0.045, so they sit at chance), the residual grows from 1.03 to 1.23 per cell, and it is
   largest on the cells just above the gap (1.8-2.5 for cells 450-498) and on the gap cells themselves
   (1.3-1.6), which receive gradient only as interior cells of larger numerals. Zeroing the gap cells'
   residual in the trained checkpoints removes the inversion (both-unseen accuracy over gap 20 goes
   32 → 92% and 9 → 93%) but does not bring back the 40-epoch precision at smaller gaps and costs seen
   accuracy, so the comparator is reading more than the gap cells' texture. Cells past the trained range
   are not residual-free either (norm about 1.0): the tied softmax trains them through the never-seen
   numerals' logits.

8. **Annealing the residual to zero does not hand the comparator the ramp route; the comparator follows
   the residual down instead.** `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal` holds the residual at full scale for 24
   epochs, scales it linearly to zero by epoch 56 and trains 24 more epochs without it. Validation loss
   stays at 0.06-0.13 down to a scale of 0.125 and 0.21-0.24 at 0.031, then jumps to 1.8-1.9 at exactly
   zero in all three seeds, worse than chance, and recovers only to 1.17 by epoch 80: the residual-free
   model is at chance on every split (test 52.6 ± 0.1%), and the same arm trained from scratch is at 0.15
   by epoch 20, so the collapsed comparator is a worse starting point than random initialisation.
   Two scale sweeps on saved checkpoints show what happened. The epoch-24 model, trained at full scale,
   is already at chance when evaluated at scale 0.3 (49% and 76% for two seeds; the residual's share of
   the normalised numeral embedding is then 0.50). The epoch-52 model, trained at scale 0.125, reads that
   scale at 99.5-99.8% (share 0.54, the same signal the earlier model could not read), scores 71-78% at
   full scale and one seed still reads 0.03 at 96%; at zero both are at chance. No weights grew to do
   this (the first block's gain on the residual subspace rises 1.4×, the residual parameter 40%, the
   generated part shrinks 6%): the readout re-tunes to whatever residual is left, one small step per
   epoch, because that is always a smaller change than building a route through the ramp. A linear
   schedule therefore never applies pressure until its last epoch, which is a step, and the step lands
   on a comparator tuned to a signal that is no longer there. The checkpoints along the way tell the more useful story (three seeds, test = seen pairs,

9. **A geometric anneal is tracked all the way down too, but one seed then found the ramp route.**

   a shared code cannot separate neighbours; the comparator can read the ramp at unit resolution once it
   has to. Its far band is 79% (both-unseen 60%): the ramp-plane curve is quadratic in k and the far

   (`grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_hold`), which keeps the small residual the comparator has tuned itself
   to read.

10. **Anneal and hold is the best reproducible configuration so far.** `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_hold`
   takes the residual geometrically from 1 to 0.01 over epochs 24-56 and keeps it there, with the best
   epoch chosen from the held phase. Over three seeds it gives 99.4 ± 0.2% on seen pairs (gap 1 at
   90.5 ± 7.5%, gaps 2-5 at 99.1 ± 1.0%: the precision of the 80-epoch runs), 97.5 ± 2.0% on the held-out
   band with both-unseen pairs at 95.0 ± 4.0% (gap 1 at 75 ± 18%, gaps 2-5 at 93.5 ± 7.9%, gaps 6-20 at
   99.6%, over 20 at 100%), and 86.6 ± 3.6% on the far band with both-unseen pairs at 73.6 ± 7.2%. Against
   the 40-epoch dropout arm that is +6 points on the held-out band and +11 points on both-unseen held-out
   pairs with +10 points at seen gap 1; against the 80-epoch dropout arm it keeps the seen precision and
   trades 10 points of far band for 22 points of held-out band. A residual one hundredth of its trained
   size, which the comparator has tuned itself to read, gives trained cells their precision without
   letting the comparator lean on texture for the coarse decisions. %%LONG%%



10. **Capacity is not the limit here.** The small model (d = 256, 4 layers, 3.5M parameters) stayed at
   chance for 15-40 epochs at learning rates 1e-4, 3e-4 and 1e-3 (warmup 300) on this data, so the tiny
   model remains the testbed.

## Where precision stands

- Seen pairs: 81% at gap 1 and 95% at gaps 2-5 with the dropout arm at 40 epochs; 92% / 99.4% at 80
  epochs.
- Both operands unseen, held-out band (interpolation): 94% at gaps 6-20 and 100% beyond at 40 epochs; gaps
  2-5 sit at 66% and gap 1 at 54%. At 80 epochs this band erodes to chance-or-inverted in two seeds of
  three.
- Both operands unseen, far band (extrapolation): 82% at gaps 6-20 and 100% beyond at 40 epochs; at 80
  epochs 99.5% at gaps 6-20, 86% at gaps 2-5 and 78% at gap 1, so neighbouring never-seen values can be
  resolved after all, in that band.
- So 40 and 80 epochs are two points on a curve, not a best and a worst: the one keeps interpolation, the
  other buys seen precision and extrapolation. Model selection currently sees only in-range validation
  pairs, so "best epoch" tracks seen loss; a validation set with its own unseen band would let early
  stopping find the compromise instead of a fixed epoch count. The structural next step is still to make
  the ramp-only route real: a residual that is strong early and annealed to zero, or a residual-free
  fine-tuning stage, so that the comparator learns to read the shared code rather than merely tolerate a
  smaller residual.
