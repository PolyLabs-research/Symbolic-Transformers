# Magnitude comparison, precision: close-pair-enriched data

Data generated by `python scripts/magnitude_experiment.py --out-dir runs/magnitude_precision --holdout 350-449
--small-gap-frac 0.4 --small-gap-max 10 --epochs 40 --seeds 1 2 3`; the later batches reuse the same data
directory. Raw numbers for every arm: `magnitude_precision.json`.

Same layout as `magnitude_offcenter.md` (values 0-499, 350-449 held out, 500-624 never seen) but 40% of
pairs are drawn with |a − b| ≤ 10, so gaps of 1-10 make up 35% of training instead of about 2%, and the
both-unseen held-out bins at small gaps hold ~1,400 pairs instead of a few dozen. 40 epochs unless stated.

Arms, all `grid_shared` with the ramp basis and a per-cell residual unless named otherwise:

| arm | residual | extra |
|---|---|---|
| `learned_untied`, `grid_linear` | – | controls from the earlier studies |
| `grid_ramp_res` | lr ×1, weight decay 0.01 | |
| `grid_ramp_res_mid` / `_fast` | lr ×3, wd 0.03 / lr ×10, wd 0.1 | stronger residual |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth`, `_mid_orth`, `_fast_orth` | as above | residual projected off the ramp plane (`--residual-orthogonal`) |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_80ep` | lr ×1, orthogonal | 80 epochs |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_drop` | lr ×1, orthogonal | half the training batches embed with the ramp alone (`--residual-dropout 0.5`) |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_cdrop` | lr ×1, orthogonal | each cell's residual row dropped independently (`--residual-dropout-cell`) |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_drop_80ep` | lr ×1, orthogonal, dropout 0.5 | 80 epochs |
| `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal` | lr ×1, orthogonal | residual scaled 1 → 0 linearly between 30% and 70% of training (`--residual-anneal 0.3 0.7`), 80 epochs; best epoch from the residual-free phase |

| `sinusoid_smooth` | – | baseline: fixed sinusoid features of k (periods 32 and up) through the same linear layer, no panels, 120 epochs |

## Reading

1. **Close-pair training buys seen precision, as intended.** grid_linear goes from 83% to 98.8% at gaps 2-5
   and holds 85.6% at gap 1; the ramp with the fast residual reaches 90.2% at gap 1 and 99.7% at gaps 2-5.
   The learned table reaches 58% / 77%.

2. **And it destroys interpolation for every fine-structure encoder.** On both-unseen held-out pairs over
   gap 20, grid_linear falls from 81.7% (uniform data) to 1.4 ± 1.6%, and the fast residual to 2.1 ± 1.4%:
   not chance, systematically inverted. Their comparators learned to read per-cell texture, and cells that
   were never trained have none (grid_linear) or only the block pattern, which reads backwards.

3. **Residual strength is a dial between the two.** With the residual kept off the magnitude plane, going
   from lr ×1 to ×3 to ×10 moves held-out accuracy 88.1 → 70.6 → 61.8% and both-unseen gap 6-20 accuracy
   81.7 → 44.6 → 22.4%, while seen gap-1 accuracy goes 80.7 → 85.9 → 86.2%. The orthogonal projection helps
   at the slow and middle settings (held-out 88.1 vs 83.4%, 70.6 vs 65.4%; far band 78.9 vs 72.1%, 73.3 vs
   65.1%); at the fast setting the residual dominates whichever way it points.

4. **Longer training is the same trade, not a way out.** 80 epochs of the slow orthogonal residual give the
   best seen precision of the study (92.0 ± 6.1% at gap 1, 99.5% at gaps 2-5, validation loss 0.037) and
   give back the unseen band: both-unseen held-out pairs at gaps 6-20 fall from 81.7% to 36.3%, and over gap
   20 from 100% to 11.9%, inverted again. The embedding has not lost magnitude (linear readout of k still
   transfers to the held-out band at Spearman 1.000 and to the far band at 0.903): the comparator stopped
   reading it. The residual simply keeps growing (per-cell norm 3.4 → 3.9 for seed 1, against 5.5 for the
   generated part).

5. **No model so far has a magnitude route through the ramp alone.** Zeroing the residual of a trained
   checkpoint (slow orthogonal, 40 or 80 epochs) drops it to chance on every pair, seen ones included
   (test 41-53%). The residual is not a refinement on top of a working shared code; it is a large part of
   every embedding and the comparator is tuned to the sum, so the "unseen" failure is the comparator
   meeting a sum whose residual half it was never trained on.

6. **Residual dropout is the best configuration so far, on every unseen measure, at no cost on seen
   pairs.** Embedding half of the training batches with the ramp alone (`grid_ramp_res_orth_drop`) gives
   test 98.6 ± 0.3% (gap 1 81.0 ± 3.8%, gaps 2-5 95.3 ± 1.7%, the same as the slow orthogonal residual),
   held-out 91.9 ± 2.2% with both-unseen pairs at 83.8 ± 4.3% (gaps 6-20 94.0 ± 2.9%, over gap 20 100%),
   and far band 88.5 ± 3.6% with both-unseen pairs at 77.6 ± 7.0% (gaps 6-20 82.4 ± 9.1%, over gap 20
   100%). The linear readout of k transfers to both bands at Spearman 1.000 and PC1 tracks k on all three.
   It does not work the way it was designed to: training loss stays near 0.46 while validation loss (residual
   intact) reaches 0.08, so the ramp-only batches sit at chance and the model learned to be indifferent to
   them rather than to read them, and zeroing the residual still gives chance. What changed is the residual
   itself: per-cell norm 1.0-1.2 instead of 3.4 (generated part 3.2 instead of 5.6), a third of the shared
   part instead of 60%, and a comparator trained on a small residual reads untrained cells' generated
   weights correctly. The per-cell variant (`_cdrop`) never trained: validation loss 0.67 after 40 epochs
   in every seed.

7. **Training the dropout arm longer trades the held-out band for the far band.** At 80 epochs
   (`grid_ramp_res_orth_drop_80ep`, validation loss 0.035 ± 0.003, best epochs 75-79) seen precision reaches
   the level of the 80-epoch run without dropout (91.7 ± 2.0% at gap 1, 99.4 ± 0.4% at gaps 2-5), and the
   far band becomes the best unseen result of the study: 96.3 ± 1.9% overall, both-unseen pairs 92.8 ± 3.8%,
   with 78.1 ± 15.5% at gap 1, 86.0 ± 10.5% at gaps 2-5 and 99.5% at gaps 6-20 (seed 1 is at 100% from gap 1
   up). The held-out band goes the other way: 75.9 ± 8.0% overall, both-unseen pairs 52.3 ± 15.6%, and two
   of three seeds invert over gap 20 (32% and 9%; the third keeps 100%). The mechanism is the one from
   point 4 in a milder form. The ramp-only batches are still not learned (training loss 0.40 against
   validation 0.045, so they sit at chance), the residual grows from 1.03 to 1.23 per cell, and it is
   largest on the cells just above the gap (1.8-2.5 for cells 450-498) and on the gap cells themselves
   (1.3-1.6), which receive gradient only as interior cells of larger numerals. Zeroing the gap cells'
   residual in the trained checkpoints removes the inversion (both-unseen accuracy over gap 20 goes
   32 → 92% and 9 → 93%) but does not bring back the 40-epoch precision at smaller gaps and costs seen
   accuracy, so the comparator is reading more than the gap cells' texture. Cells past the trained range
   are not residual-free either (norm about 1.0): the tied softmax trains them through the never-seen
   numerals' logits.

8. **Annealing the residual to zero does not hand the comparator the ramp route; the comparator follows
   the residual down instead.** `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal` holds the residual at full scale for 24
   epochs, scales it linearly to zero by epoch 56 and trains 24 more epochs without it. Validation loss
   stays at 0.06-0.13 down to a scale of 0.125 and 0.21-0.24 at 0.031, then jumps to 1.8-1.9 at exactly
   zero in all three seeds, worse than chance, and recovers only to 1.17 by epoch 80: the residual-free
   model is at chance on every split (test 52.6 ± 0.1%), and the same arm trained from scratch is at 0.15
   by epoch 20, so the collapsed comparator is a worse starting point than random initialisation.
   Two scale sweeps on saved checkpoints show what happened. The epoch-24 model, trained at full scale,
   is already at chance when evaluated at scale 0.3 (49% and 76% for two seeds; the residual's share of
   the normalised numeral embedding is then 0.50). The epoch-52 model, trained at scale 0.125, reads that
   scale at 99.5-99.8% (share 0.54, the same signal the earlier model could not read), scores 71-78% at
   full scale and one seed still reads 0.03 at 96%; at zero both are at chance. No weights grew to do
   this (the first block's gain on the residual subspace rises 1.4×, the residual parameter 40%, the
   generated part shrinks 6%): the readout re-tunes to whatever residual is left, one small step per
   epoch, because that is always a smaller change than building a route through the ramp. A linear
   schedule therefore never applies pressure until its last epoch, which is a step, and the step lands
   on a comparator tuned to a signal that is no longer there. The checkpoints along the way tell the more useful story (three seeds, test = seen pairs,

   recovers (validation loss 0.81 at epoch 64, 0.20 at epoch 72) and its best residual-free checkpoint,

   at gaps 2-5) and 99.7% on the held-out band with both-unseen pairs at 99.3%, including 91% at gap 1
   and 100% from gap 2 up: a model whose every numeral embedding is generated from two shared directions
   resolves neighbouring unseen values. That retracts the claim in the earlier version of this note that
   a shared code cannot separate neighbours; the comparator can read the ramp at unit resolution once it
   has to. Its far band is 79% (both-unseen 60%): the ramp-plane curve is quadratic in k and the far
   values sit on a part of it the comparator never saw. Seeds 1 and 3 did not recover in 24 epochs
   (validation loss 1.03 and 1.01 at epoch 80, still falling), and the phase is handicapped: the cosine
   schedule has the learning rate at 6e-5 falling to its 3e-5 floor from epoch 56 on, a fifth to a tenth
   of the rate the model learned the task at. Two follow-ups are running: the same anneal pinned to
   epochs 24-56 of a 120-epoch run (`grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_geo_long`), which gives the residual-free
   phase 64 epochs starting at 1.9e-4, and an anneal that stops at 0.01 and holds
   (`grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_hold`), which keeps the small residual the comparator has tuned itself
   to read.

10. **Anneal and hold is the best reproducible configuration so far.** `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_hold`
   takes the residual geometrically from 1 to 0.01 over epochs 24-56 and keeps it there, with the best
   epoch chosen from the held phase. Over three seeds it gives 99.4 ± 0.2% on seen pairs (gap 1 at
   90.5 ± 7.5%, gaps 2-5 at 99.1 ± 1.0%: the precision of the 80-epoch runs), 97.5 ± 2.0% on the held-out
   band with both-unseen pairs at 95.0 ± 4.0% (gap 1 at 75 ± 18%, gaps 2-5 at 93.5 ± 7.9%, gaps 6-20 at
   99.6%, over 20 at 100%), and 86.6 ± 3.6% on the far band with both-unseen pairs at 73.6 ± 7.2%. Against
   the 40-epoch dropout arm that is +6 points on the held-out band and +11 points on both-unseen held-out
   pairs with +10 points at seen gap 1; against the 80-epoch dropout arm it keeps the seen precision and
   trades 10 points of far band for 22 points of held-out band. A residual one hundredth of its trained
   size, which the comparator has tuned itself to read, gives trained cells their precision without
   letting the comparator lean on texture for the coarse decisions.

11. **With a long, properly paced residual-free phase, the step to zero still succeeds in one seed of
   three, and that seed is the best model of the study.** `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_geo_long` runs the
   same geometric anneal at epochs 24-56 of a 120-epoch run, so the residual-free phase has 64 epochs
   and starts at a learning rate of 1.9e-4 instead of 6e-5. Seed 2 recovers again (validation loss 0.19
   at epoch 73, 0.08 by epoch 120) and its residual-free model scores 99.9% on seen pairs (95% at gap 1,
   100% at gaps 2-5), 99.2% on the held-out band with both-unseen pairs at 98% (79% at gap 1, 100% from
   gap 2 up) and 89.3% on the far band with both-unseen pairs at 79%. Seeds 1 and 3 fall to a validation
   loss of 0.70-0.71, the entropy of a coin, by epoch 80 and stay there: after the step they settle on
   predicting each operator's prior and the gradient of that fixed point does not point toward the ramp.
   Their best residual-free checkpoints score 51.8% and 52.5% on seen pairs and 49.7-49.5% on the

12. **A learning-rate re-warm-up at the step makes recovery less likely, not more.**
   `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_geo_rewarm` is the 80-epoch geometric anneal with the schedule restarted at
   epoch 56 (`--residual-anneal-rewarm 100`: 100 warm-up steps from 3e-6 back to the base rate of 3e-4,
   then cosine decay to 3e-5 by epoch 80, instead of the 6e-5 → 3e-5 tail the plain run had). All three
   seeds, the one that recovered in both plain geometric runs included, go from the collapse (validation
   loss 1.26-1.35 at epoch 56) to the coin-flip fixed point in twelve epochs (0.77 at epoch 64, 0.71-0.72
   from epoch 72 on) and stay there; their best residual-free checkpoints are at chance on every split.
   Validation loss after the step, the re-warm-up seeds against the plain geometric seeds:

   | epoch | re-warm-up s1 / s2 / s3 | plain geometric s1 / s2 / s3 |
   |---|---|---|
   | 56 | 1.29 / 1.35 / 1.26 | 1.42 / 1.38 / 1.39 |
   | 64 | 0.77 / 0.77 / 0.75 | 1.22 / 0.81 / 1.19 |
   | 72 | 0.72 / 0.72 / 0.71 | 1.12 / 0.20 / 1.10 |
   | 80 | 0.71 / 0.71 / 0.71 | 1.03 / 0.20 / 1.01 |

   From the collapsed state the steepest descent is toward predicting each operator's prior, and a high
   learning rate takes that path fast and settles in its flat bottom; the slow cosine tail is what let
   seed 2 drift along a longer route to the ramp, and the two seeds that did not recover were descending
   the same slow slope (1.4 → 1.0) when training ended rather than sitting at the fixed point. The step
   to zero is a cliff either way; a higher rate only decides which bottom the model reaches.

13. **Continuing the anneal below the hold floor removes the cliff: the residual fades into float noise
   and the comparator crosses to the ramp on its own, in every seed.** `grid_ramp_res_orth_anneal_deep`
   (120 epochs, `--residual-anneal-stages`) holds the residual at 0.01 from epoch 56 to 68 and then takes
   it geometrically to 1e-6 by epoch 108, a decade per ten epochs, and holds. Validation loss stays at
   0.05-0.14 down to a scale of 6e-4 and then rises gradually rather than jumping: to 0.12-0.19 at 1e-4,
   to 0.14-0.48 at 1.6e-5 (seed 3 touches 0.68, chance, at 1e-4), and back down to 0.09-0.16 by the time
   the scale reaches 1e-6. The crossing sits where the residual's share of a numeral embedding is about
   1e-5 to 1e-4 (a residual row of norm 5 scaled by 1e-5 adds 5e-5 to an embedding of norm 5), two to three
   decades above float32's relative resolution, so "below float32 readability" is not the right description;
   what sets the floor (the number format, the optimiser's epsilon, the LayerNorm epsilon of 1e-5, or the
   batch gradient noise) is open and is the first question in `plan_distinctive_result.md`. What is
   established is that the comparator's readout, which re-tunes to any signal it can read, runs out of
   signal there. Because readability is lost over twenty epochs rather than in one step, the comparator
   rebuilds its route gradually and lands on the ramp instead of on the operator prior. The final models
   are residual-free in effect: evaluating them with the scale set to exactly zero changes no number
   (seed 1: 99.0% / 98.2% / 72.3% on test / held-out / far at 1e-6 and at 0; seed 2: 99.8% / 98.6% /
   82.8%; seed 3: 100% / 96.5% / 78%). The best epoch is chosen across the final hold (epochs 108-120;
   the hold is noisy, with single-epoch spikes to 0.46 and 1.47 in two seeds, so this matters): epochs

   2-5 at 95%, gaps 6-20 at 100%) and 77.7 ± 4.3% on the far band with both-unseen pairs at 64%: the

14. **The scaffold is necessary: the ramp alone cannot be learned from scratch, and a smooth
   fixed-feature code does not interpolate.** Two baselines on the same data and budget (120 epochs,
   three seeds). `grid_ramp`, the generated embedding with no residual ever, never leaves chance:
   validation loss 0.69-0.70 at every epoch in every seed (best 0.693-0.694, the entropy of a coin; test accuracy 51.8-52.6%, held-out and far bands at 48-51%), while a linear readout of k from its numeral embeddings is perfect on every band, so the magnitude is there and the comparator never learns to read it. The same code the deep-annealed
   comparator reads at unit resolution is unlearnable without the scaffold, because at initialisation
   the ramp's one-step rotation of the embedding direction is too small a signal to climb out of the
   plateau, whereas the residual gives a large per-cell signal to learn the task on, and fading it hands
   the trained comparator over to the ramp a step at a time. `sinusoid_smooth`, fixed sinusoid features
   of k with periods of 32 and up through the same linear layer, is the opposite failure: it fits seen
   pairs perfectly (100% on every seen gap in every seed, validation loss 0.002-0.012) because the features identify each value, and that is all the
   comparator learns: 70.7 ± 2.6% on the held-out band with both-unseen pairs at 46% and inverted beyond gap 20 (7-33%),
   and 59.5 ± 3.6% on the far band (both-unseen 64%), the pathology of `grid_linear` in point 2 with a
   different code. Against these the deep anneal's 99.6% / 97.8% / 77.7% is not a
   curiosity of the schedule but the only route found to a comparator that reads magnitude from a
   shared code.



10. **Capacity is not the limit here.** The small model (d = 256, 4 layers, 3.5M parameters) stayed at
   chance for 15-40 epochs at learning rates 1e-4, 3e-4 and 1e-3 (warmup 300) on this data, so the tiny
   model remains the testbed.

15. **Correction (2026-10-16): the residual also carried the operator distinction, and points 5, 8-13
   conflate the two.** The operator and answer tokens are grid panels through the same encoder and the same
   residual, and under the ramp basis their generated parts are nearly identical (post-LayerNorm difference
   0.005, cosine 0.999996), so the operator distinction lives in the residual. Re-evaluating the saved
   models with only the numeral rows re-scaled (`evaluate_magnitude.py --residual-scale-rows numerals`,
   details in `withdrawal_routes.md`) shows that the hold model already reads numerals from the ramp alone
   in two seeds of three (98.8 / 98.3 / 88.6 and 99.6 / 100.0 / 81.9 with the numeral residual at zero;
   the third seed keeps two points), that the plain 40-epoch model is at chance without its numeral
   residual and the 80-epoch dropout models lose their answer-token readout (seed 1 at chance when
   restricted to TRUE/FALSE, seed 2 partial, seed 3 chance), and that what was observed between scales
   0.01 and 1e-6 (the crossing, the cliff at zero, the seed-dependent recovery, the re-warm-up failure)
   mostly concerns the comparator losing and relearning a 0.3% difference between two token embeddings.
   What survives: the numeral handover to the ramp happens in the anneal from 1 to 0.01 (point 10's hold
   arm), the dropout and full-scale comparators of the old architecture do depend on the numeral
   residual, and the baselines of point 14 are confounded in the other direction (a ramp-only model has no
   way to tell the operators apart). Re-run with `--grid-numerals-only` (operators and answers as learned
   rows), the ramp alone from scratch learns the task directly, three seeds: 99.3 ± 0.2% on seen pairs
   (gap 1 at 90%), 96.0 ± 0.5% on the held-out band with both-unseen pairs at 92%, and 89.8 ± 0.8% on the

   the readability floor were compensating for the operators sharing the residual; `withdrawal_routes.md`

## Where precision stands

**See point 15 before reading this section. The residual-free status of the deep and recovered models
holds for every row; what was misread is why the fade was needed and where its floor lies (the operator
tokens shared the residual). The recommended configuration is now the numerals-only ramp with learned
operator rows, `--embedding grid_shared --shared-basis ramp --grid-numerals-only`, with no residual; the
"scaffold is necessary" bullet below is withdrawn.**


| configuration | seen gap 1 / gaps 2-5 | held-out band, both unseen (gap 1 / 2-5 / 6-20) | far band, both unseen |
|---|---|---|---|
| dropout 0.5, 40 epochs | 81 / 95 | 84% (54 / 66 / 94) | 78% |
| dropout 0.5, 80 epochs | 92 / 99 | 52% (50 / 51 / 53) | 93% |
| anneal to 0.01 and hold, 80 epochs | 90 / 99 | 95% (75 / 94 / 100) | 74% |
| anneal to zero, geometric, the seeds that recover (2 of 6) | 94-95 / 99-100 | 98-99% (79-91 / 100 / 100) | 60-79% |
| anneal below the floor to 1e-6, 120 epochs, 3 of 3 seeds (residual-free in effect) | 92 / 99 | 95% (70 / 95 / 100) | 64% |
| baseline: the ramp alone from scratch, 120 epochs | chance | chance | chance |
| baseline: smooth sinusoid features of k, 120 epochs | 100 / 100 | 46% (54 / 54 / 48) | 64% |

- Seen pairs: 90-92% at gap 1 and 99% at gaps 2-5 are now routine (hold and deep-anneal arms, three
  seeds each); 95% / 100% from the recovered residual-free models.
- Both operands unseen, held-out band: 95% with the hold arm and 95% with the deep anneal (residual-free,
  every seed), 98-99% in the residual-free seeds that recovered from the step, with gap 1 at 70-91% and
  everything from gap 2 up at 95-100%. The earlier claim that a shared code cannot separate
  neighbouring unseen values was wrong: the comparator resolves the ramp at unit resolution when the
  residual is small or gone.
- Both operands unseen, far band: 64-79% with the hold, recovered and deep-annealed models, 93% with the
  80-epoch dropout arm, which is the one place the dropout arm still leads. Extrapolation and
  interpolation are not yet maximised by the same model.
- The scaffold is necessary. The ramp alone cannot be learned from scratch in 120 epochs (chance in every
  seed), and a smooth fixed-feature code learns seen pairs perfectly and inverts on unseen ones, so no
  baseline reaches the faded model's held-out band. The residual is never a route of its own: zeroing it
  in any model trained with it at full strength gives chance, and the comparator follows it down to any
  scale it can read. There are two ways to a ramp-only comparator. The step to zero works in a third of seeds, and a learning-rate re-warm-up at
  the step makes that worse (0 of 3), because the fast route from the collapse is to the operator prior.
  Continuing the anneal below the hold floor works in every seed: between 1e-4 and 1e-5 the residual
  drops below what the comparator can read (what sets that floor is open; it is well above float32's
  resolution), readability is lost over twenty epochs instead of in one step, and the comparator rebuilds
  on the ramp; the resulting models give the same numbers with the residual at 1e-6 and at exactly zero.
- Recommended configurations: `--residual-anneal 0.3 0.7 --residual-anneal-floor 0.01
  --residual-anneal-hold` for the best overall numbers (a small residual stays), and
  `--residual-anneal-stages 0.2:1,0.4667:0.01,0.5667:0.01,0.9:1e-6` over 120 epochs for a
  residual-free model (same seen precision and held-out band, weaker far band).
- Next: the far band is where residual-free models trail (64-79% on both-unseen pairs against 93% for the
  80-epoch dropout arm), so extrapolation past the trained range is the open problem, and a validation
  set with its own unseen band would let best-epoch selection see generalisation at all.
